# 09. Digital packs in the UK's new licence-exempt 1785–1805 MHz mic band by frequency translation, received by one SDR

**One sentence.** Put the system where wireless microphones are supposed to be (1785-1805 MHz, licence-exempt in the UK since 29 April 2026 at 50 mW EIRP body-worn) by translating a cheap radio's output into the band with a doubler or a mixer, and receive every pack at once with one 20 MHz software-defined radio.

**How the air works.** FDMA like v1 but continuous and narrow: each pack transmits a 300-400 kbps GFSK (or 4-FSK) stream on its own ~500 kHz carrier, 32 carriers in 16-18 MHz, no slots, no superframe. Three ways to make a cheap chip transmit at 1.79 GHz: (a) a sub-GHz transceiver (TI CC1200 at 1.2 Mbps 4-FSK, Semtech SX1262 at 300 kbps GFSK) tuned to 892.5-902.5 MHz feeding a passive frequency doubler (two Schottky diodes or an active doubler), a 1.8 GHz band-pass filter and a small PA to 50 mW; doubling doubles the deviation, which the receiver simply expects; (b) a 2.4 GHz radio (nRF52) mixed down with a 615-ish MHz LO (ADF4351 or a cheap VCO) through an image filter; (c) [verify] whether nRF9151's DECT NR+ band support reaches 1790+ MHz (DECT-2020 band 9 is sometimes listed as 1790-1825 MHz; if the modem can be driven there, the chip from idea 08 lands directly in the PMSE band). Downlink: the hub transmits a low-rate beacon on one carrier with the same translation chain, or on 2.4 GHz with the pack's host radio (no translation needed for a receive-only path at 2.4 GHz).

**How the receiver works.** One SDR covering 20 MHz at 1.8 GHz: ADALM-Pluto (£190, 56 MHz with the hack, USB 2.0 limits streaming to ~5 MSPS unless the DSP runs on its Zynq), AntSDR E200 (£160-200, Zynq 7020, Gigabit Ethernet, streams 20+ MSPS), LimeSDR Mini 2.0 (£200), HackRF (£220, 8-bit). The host runs 32 polyphase channelisers + GFSK demodulators (ice9-bluetooth-sniffer already demodulates dozens of BLE channels from one SDR on a CPU/GPU). Marginal cost per channel: zero. A second SDR or antenna port for diversity.

**Capacity maths.** 32 × 500 kHz = 16 MHz in a 20 MHz band, 2 MHz of guard. Continuous FDMA has no slot overhead and no beacon dependence for timing. Beyond 32 the band is full: ~40 mics maximum. Spectral efficiency 0.6 bit/s/Hz; 4-FSK or a coded 8-FSK doubles it to ~64-80 mics but costs sensitivity.

**Latency budget.** Continuous streaming: codec 300 → packetise 1000 (1 ms frames, FEC inside) → air (serial, continuous) ~0 wait → SDR ADC/FPGA 100 → USB/Ethernet buffers 500-2000 [the crux: SDR host libraries default to 10+ ms buffers; minimum achievable with small transfers ~1 ms] → channeliser + demod 500 → host 300 → out 250 ≈ 3-4.5 ms.

**Reliability story.** Nothing in the hostile test can legally transmit in 1785-1805 except other wireless mics (now licence-exempt too, so a visiting band's analog mics at 200 kHz each could appear; the SDR sees the whole band and the downlink reassigns carriers). No Wi-Fi, no Bluetooth, no DMX, no phones (DCS 1800 uplink stops at 1785 and downlink starts at 1805; the band is the duplex gap, which is why it is for mics). Continuous FDMA's weakness is a single bad carrier (multipath null); mitigate with interleaved FEC and a slow carrier change via downlink.

**Cost.** Pack: CC1200 £5 (or SX1262 £3) + doubler/filter/PA/matching £6-10 + MCU £2.50 + codec £3 + misc £8 + jack £3 + case £2 ≈ £30-34 ex cell (at/over budget; the translation chain is the cost). Hub: SDR £180 + host £110 + filter/LNA £20 = £310 fixed (>25 % over £150: tripwire; but £0 per channel, so 32 channels at £310 beats the £470 budget line). 32-channel ≈ £1,300.

**UK legality.** IR2030/35/3 (S.I. 2026/396): 1785-1805 MHz licence-exempt, 50 mW EIRP body-worn, EN 300 422. The doubler's fundamental at 895 MHz falls in the GSM900 uplink and must be suppressed below the spurious limit (-36 dBm): needs ≥ 50 dB of filtering, which a DCS1800 band filter plus a notch provides on paper and must be measured. Spurious/harmonic measurements are the main compliance work.

**What v1 code or analysis it reuses.** Nearly all of v1's air-interface thinking (continuous FDMA, one carrier per pack, ADPCM in 1 ms frames) and its codec; v1's "one receiver radio per pack" problem disappears because the SDR is the receiver for all of them.

**Riskiest assumption** and the cheapest experiment. That a hobby-grade translation chain can be made clean enough (fundamental and image suppression, phase noise) at £6-10 per pack. Experiment (< £120): one CC1200 dev module (£25) + a diode doubler + a 1.8 GHz SAW/ceramic filter (£5) + TinySA Ultra to look at the output; then receive it on an RTL-SDR with an R828D tuner (reaches ~1.8 GHz [verify]).

**Why it might be wrong.** Hand-built RF multiplier chains are exactly the kind of thing that works on one board and not the next; the owner has no RF background and the agents cannot hold a soldering iron. If nRF9151 cannot be driven into the band, the pack is £34 and the compliance measurements are the hardest on the board. The SDR receiver has no per-channel cost but its host latency is the least-proven number here.
