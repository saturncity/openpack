# 05. Raw 802.11 frames at 5 GHz: £3 Wi-Fi SoC packs, one £15 monitor-mode dongle as the receiver

**One sentence.** Packs are ESP32-C5 (dual-band Wi-Fi 6) modules that inject raw 802.11 data frames on a 5 GHz channel in a TDMA schedule without associating to anything; the receiver is any Linux Wi-Fi adapter in monitor mode, so one £15 USB dongle receives all 32 mics at once.

**How the air works.** 5 GHz, 20 MHz OFDM channel at a fixed rate (MCS/legacy 24-54 Mbps), broadcast data frames with no ACK and no retry (esp_wifi_80211_tx). A hub beacon (also a raw frame, sent by an ESP32 or by the Linux side via packet injection) every 2 ms carries timing, slot map, control. Packs listen to the beacon with the radio in promiscuous mode. Why 5 GHz: the audience's phones mostly idle on 2.4 GHz for Bluetooth and hotspots; Wi-Fi 6 OFDM frames at 24-54 Mbps are short; and 802.11 devices that hear our preambles defer to us (CSMA works for us, not only against us). Band choice: 5150-5250 MHz (200 mW EIRP indoor, no DFS) or 5725-5850 (200 mW with DFS in the UK since 2017 [verify]; 25 mW as SRD under EN 300 440 without DFS).

**How the receiver works.** A Linux mini PC (or the laptop) with one or two USB Wi-Fi adapters whose driver supports monitor mode (MediaTek MT7612U/MT7921AU, ~£15-25), set to the channel; a userspace program reads frames via libpcap/nl80211 and reassembles audio. Second adapter on a second channel for a repair copy. Base station hardware is therefore £30-50, no custom PCB at all. For the prototype, ESP32-C5 dev boards in promiscuous mode can be the receiver.

**Capacity maths.** 68-byte payload + 24-byte MAC header + 4 FCS = 96 bytes; at 24 Mbps: 20 µs preamble + 4 µs SIG + 9 symbols × 4 µs = 60 µs; plus DIFS 34 µs (no backoff when the medium was idle) ≈ 95 µs per slot → ~20 slots per 2 ms frame per channel → 32 mics single copy on 2 channels (2 dongles), with repair on 4. One 20 MHz channel carries 16 mics; the black box's 6-16 fits one dongle.

**Latency budget.** Codec 300 → frame 2000 → ESP32 Wi-Fi TX path (queue, DIFS) 150-400 [measure] → air 60 → dongle + driver + libpcap 300-800 [measure] → host 300 → out 250 ≈ 3.4-4.1 ms single copy; repair at T/2 +1000 → 4.4-5.1 ms. A 1 ms frame halves slots per channel (4 dongles) and gives ~3 ms.

**Reliability story.** 802.11 coexistence: a phone that hears our preamble defers; we defer to it, so a long frame from a neighbour (1500 bytes at MCS0 = 2 ms) can push our slot by up to 2 ms, which is the main threat; mitigate by choosing a channel with no AP in the venue and by the repair copy on another channel. OFDM with convolutional coding (rate 1/2 at 6/12/24 Mbps) is far more robust to multipath than GFSK. No ACK/retry means loss is visible and concealable. Hostile-test exposure: a 5 GHz hotspot at 1 m on our channel is the worst case; the beacon moves the system to another channel (there are ~19 of them) within 0.5 s. Re-acquire: pack scans for the beacon on the channel list.

**Cost.** Pack: ESP32-C5 module £3 + codec £3 + preamp/power/misc £8 + jack £3 + case £2 ≈ £19 ex cell (Wi-Fi TX draws ~300 mA bursts: ~3 % duty → fine for 16 h). Base station: mini PC £80-110 (or £0 with the laptop) + 2-4 dongles £30-100 = £110-210 fixed, £0 per channel. 32-channel ≈ £800.

**UK legality.** EN 301 893 for 5 GHz RLAN (the chip's own compliance covers the PHY: ESP32-C5 modules carry CE/RED). 5150-5250 indoor only, 200 mW EIRP, no DFS; 5250-5350 and 5470-5725 need DFS, which raw-frame injection does not implement, so avoid them. Measure channel occupancy and EIRP.

**What v1 code or analysis it reuses.** Codec, UI, host audio path, concealment. Nothing on the radio side.

**Riskiest assumption** and the cheapest experiment. That the ESP32-C5 TX path and a Linux monitor-mode dongle have bounded, sub-millisecond latency and that raw-frame reception at 20 m is ≥ 99 % frame-success in a crowded 5 GHz room. Experiment (< £80): two ESP32-C5 dev boards (£8 each) + one MT7612U dongle; GPIO toggle at TX request and at libpcap receipt on a logic analyser; loss count over an hour with a 5 GHz hotspot at 1 m.

**Why it might be wrong.** Wi-Fi silicon is designed for throughput, not timing: firmware queues, power-save and rate-control state machines may add jitter that no open driver can remove. Phones increasingly default to 5 GHz hotspots. Promiscuous RX on the pack to hear beacons costs 100 mA continuously (kills the 16 h runtime) unless the beacon is on a known schedule and the radio sleeps between. Raw-frame injection relies on vendor SDK functions that may change.
