# 07. Ultra-wideband (IEEE 802.15.4z, Qorvo DW3000): the car-key and AirTag radio carrying audio

**One sentence.** Use the 500 MHz-wide impulse radio that Apple, Samsung and the car industry have driven to ~£5 silicon (DW3000/DW3110/DW3220, NXP SR040), which is by construction immune to narrowband Wi-Fi and Bluetooth interference and gives centimetre positioning of every actor for free.

**How the air works.** 6.5 GHz (channel 5) and 8 GHz (channel 9), 500 MHz bandwidth, mean EIRP limited to -41.3 dBm/MHz (about -14 dBm total). HRP UWB PHY at 6.8 Mbps data rate, 64 MHz PRF, preamble codes 9-12. TDMA: hub sends a beacon per 2 ms superframe (also used as the TDoA ranging reference); each pack sends one frame in its slot. Two channels = two parallel schedules. Join via a contention slot. UWB's STS/ranging features are a bonus: every frame's arrival time at 2-3 anchors places the actor to ~30 cm, which can drive automatic panning, follow-spot cues, or "whose mic is this" in the booth.

**How the receiver works.** 2-4 DWM3000-class modules (£10-15 each as modules, £5-7 as chips) on a board, each over SPI to an MCU (nRF52840/RP2040), host as idea 01. Antennas are small ceramic chips or PCB monopoles (UWB antennas are tiny at 6-8 GHz). Cheap because the radio is a commodity consumer part and the receiver count is 2-4, not 8-14.

**Capacity maths.** Frame on air at 6.8 Mbps: 64-symbol preamble ≈ 64 µs + SFD 8 µs + PHR 22 µs + 68 bytes 80 µs ≈ 175 µs (≈ 145 µs with a 32-symbol preamble); turnaround/guard 30 µs → ~200 µs per slot → 10 slots per channel per 2 ms frame; 2 channels → 20 mics single copy. 32 mics needs T ≈ 3.2 ms (fits P3, see below) or two receivers per channel with different preamble codes receiving simultaneously (code separation of overlapping UWB packets is poor; treat as not available). Black box (6-16 mics) fits one channel with a repair copy on the other. Beyond 32: UK has only the two channels above 6 GHz (3.1-4.8 GHz needs LDC/DAA mitigation), so capacity is spectrum-capped at ~20 per 2 ms. This is UWB's hard limit.

**Latency budget.** Codec 300 → frame 2000 (3200 for 32 mics) → air 175 → SPI 100 → USB/host 800 → out 250 ≈ 3.6 ms (4.8 ms at 32 mics, no repair copy possible inside 5 ms at that count).

**Reliability story.** Narrowband interferers (Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, DMX, phones) fall below the UWB noise floor after despreading: 500 MHz vs 20 MHz gives ~14 dB processing margin against a Wi-Fi channel. Wi-Fi 6E (5925-6425 MHz in the UK) overlaps the lower part of channel 5 (6240-6740); channel 9 (7737-8237) is clean. Multipath is resolved, not suffered. The threat is link budget: -14 dBm EIRP and ~-105 dBm sensitivity give ~90 dB path budget; at 6.5 GHz, 25 m line-of-sight is ~77 dB, body shadowing 10-20 dB; the margin is thin at the back of a large stage. Re-acquire is sub-100 ms (packs are synced to the beacon). Battery: TX ~100 mA for 175 µs per 2 ms, RX for the beacon ~100 mA for 200 µs → ~20 mA average, fine.

**Cost.** Pack: DW3000 chip £6 (module £12) + MCU £2.50 + codec £3 + misc £8 + jack £3 + case £2 ≈ £24.50-30.50 ex cell. Hub fixed: host £110 + 2 modules £25 = £135; a 3rd/4th anchor for positioning +£25; £0.80 per channel. 32-channel ≈ £950-1,100.

**UK legality.** IR2030 / EN 302 065-1 (generic UWB): -41.3 dBm/MHz mean EIRP in 6-8.5 GHz, no duty-cycle or mitigation requirement there; the chip's reference designs are built to it. Measure mean PSD with a spectrum analyser that reaches 8.5 GHz (TinySA Ultra+ reaches 7.3 GHz [verify]; channel 9 may need borrowed equipment).

**What v1 code or analysis it reuses.** Codec, UI, host, concealment; v1's scheduler concept.

**Riskiest assumption** and the cheapest experiment. Range through bodies at -14 dBm EIRP in a 20 m room. Experiment (< £80): two Makerfabs/Qorvo DWM3000 dev boards (£30-40 each) at 6.8 Mbps, 1 kHz frames, walk the venue with the pack behind the torso, count losses.

**Why it might be wrong.** The power limit is brutal (6 dB less than a Bluetooth beacon); UWB in phones works at 1-10 m, not 25 m through a cast. Two channels cap the system at ~20 mics per 2 ms, so 32 mics costs latency and there is no road past 32. UWB chips are sold under NDA-heavy ecosystems (Qorvo's are open enough; NXP's are not) and module prices are not yet nRF-cheap.
