# 01. TDMA on commodity GFSK radios, beacon downlink (the reference design)

**One sentence.** Every pack transmits one 1-2 ms audio frame in its own time slot on one of a handful of 2 Mbps/4 Mbps GFSK carriers; the hub is 4-8 cheap radio modules and a small Linux host; a beacon from each hub radio carries timing, slot assignments and control.

**How the air works.** 2.4 GHz, Nordic-style GFSK (BLE 2M PHY framing or the proprietary 4 Mbps mode on nRF54L15). Superframe T = 2 ms. Each hub radio owns one carrier and sends a beacon at the top of the superframe (timing, 32-slot map, per-pack trim/mute, ACK bits). The remaining ~1.9 ms is split into slots of about 250 µs at 2 Mbps (46 bytes on air = 184 µs + 40 µs ramp + guard) or about 150 µs at 4 Mbps. A pack with no slot listens for any beacon, sends a join request in a contention slot, and is given a carrier and slot. Channel hopping is optional: the same scheduler can run as TSCH (6TiSCH / WirelessHART style): slot number + a hop list from the beacon selects the carrier, so a pack never sits on a bad channel for long. This is what the industrial world does for deterministic wireless and it is the shape of Shure ULX-D.

**How the receiver works.** 4-8 nRF52840 or nRF54L15 modules (E73/Holyiot class, £3-6 each) on one carrier board, each over USB or SPI to a host (Raspberry Pi 5 or any mini PC, £50-80; or the laptop itself). Host merges slots into channels, runs concealment, presents USB Audio Class 2 or AES67. Cheap because each module is a complete radio+MCU and the board holds no RF besides 50 Ω traces to module antennas or U.FL.

**Capacity maths.** Payload per pack: 32 kHz × 8-bit ADPCM = 32 bytes/ms, so 64 bytes per 2 ms frame + 4 header = 68 bytes; on air at 2 Mbps ≈ 46 bytes in a 1 ms variant or 76 bytes (304 µs + 50 µs) in the 2 ms variant → ~5 slots per carrier per 2 ms frame single copy. 32 mics single copy = 7 carriers; with a repair copy at T/2 on a different carrier = 13 carriers (too many). At 4 Mbps: ~10 slots per carrier → 4 carriers single copy, 7 with repair. Spectrum: 1.5-2 MHz per carrier, 8-14 MHz total, placed in the gaps left by Wi-Fi channels 1/6/11. Beyond 32 mics: add carriers (modules) until the 2.4 GHz band runs out (~40 carriers theoretical, ~20 realistic with Wi-Fi present).

**Latency budget.** Codec ADC group delay 300 µs (low-latency filter mode) → frame fill 2000 µs → slot wait (constant, by aligning the pack's frame boundary to its slot) 50 µs → air 300 µs → hub radio DMA + USB FS 500 µs → host mix/conceal 300 µs → UAC2 out 250 µs. Single copy ≈ 3.7 ms. Repair copy at T/2 adds 1000 µs → 4.7 ms. Tight but inside 5 ms; a 1 ms superframe gives 2.7/3.2 ms at the price of twice the slot overhead.

**Reliability story.** Repair copy on a second carrier at T/2; carriers chosen by a hub-side spectrum scan before the show and re-planned via beacon between numbers; hopping variant if the fixed plan fails. Loss of a beacon for 3 frames → pack free-wheels on its crystal (±20 ppm = 40 µs per 2 s, within guard) and re-syncs on the next. Walk-out → re-join in ≤ 0.5 s via a contention slot every frame. Hostile test: a hotspot at 1 m on Wi-Fi ch 6 occupies 2426-2448 MHz; both carriers for one pack must be outside that; the scan does it. The weak point is a phone hopping to our carrier mid-frame: that is what the repair copy is for.

**Cost.** Pack: nRF52840 module £4 + codec (TLV320ADC3101 or similar) £3 + preamp/bias/misc £4 + regulators/charger £3 + PCB/passives £5 + jack £3 + case print £2 ≈ £24 ex cell. Hub fixed: host £60 + PSU/case/Ethernet £30 + carrier board £20 = £110; per carrier module £5 → 7 carriers £35; per channel ≈ £1.10 (budget £10). 32-channel: 32 × £24 + £145 ≈ £915 (budget £1,500).

**UK legality.** EN 300 328 v2.2.2. Pack at ≤ 100 mW EIRP as non-adaptive equipment: medium utilisation = (P/100 mW) × duty cycle ≤ 10 %; duty cycle per pack ≈ 15 % at 20 dBm needs reduction to ≤ 10 % or use 10 mW; at 10 dBm any duty cycle is allowed. Measure duty cycle and PSD with a TinySA Ultra (£100).

**What v1 code or analysis it reuses.** ADPCM codec and corpus, Zephyr radio driver skeleton, bsim scenarios (re-gated to the shipped config), audio clock work (nRF5340 HFCLKAUDIO fine tuning), booth UI, USB audio host code, battery/charger analysis.

**Riskiest assumption** and the cheapest experiment. That 4-7 radios can hold slot timing to ±20 µs across a USB-connected host and that the whole chain stays under 5 ms. Experiment (< £100): two nRF52840 DKs + one dongle; measure mic-in GPIO to host-out GPIO with a logic analyser (£15 Saleae clone), with a third board generating 30 fake slots.

**Why it might be wrong.** It is v1's radio family again, and 2.4 GHz is the band the hostile test is designed to break. Seven to thirteen radios is a lot of USB endpoints and timing to coordinate; the repair copy halves spectral efficiency; and the per-pack duty cycle at full power rubs against EN 300 328's medium utilisation rule.
