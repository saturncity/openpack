# ADR-0015: Region UK first: EN 300 328 non-adaptive at 9 dBm EIRP; #76 channel plan
Status: accepted, with an R1 stop-and-ask (below). 2026-10-31. Issue #80 (also #76). Replaces the Q8 default.
- **Context:** The owner is in the UK. Q8 assumed a US owner. The UK rules are Ofcom **IR 2030 v1.17** (Oct 2026), under the Radio Equipment Regulations 2017 reg. 6(2), with ETSI **EN 300 328 V2.2.2** as the designated standard. IR2030/7/1 (wideband data, 2400–2483.5 MHz) allows 100 mW EIRP and 10 mW/MHz for non-FHSS. EN 300 328 adds: 4.3.2.2 EIRP ≤ 20 dBm; **4.3.2.3 PSD ≤ 10 dBm/MHz**; 4.3.2.4 for non-adaptive at ≥ 10 dBm, Tx-sequence ≤ 10 ms and **Tx-gap ≥ max(sequence, 3.5 ms)**; 4.3.2.5 MU = P/100 mW × DC ≤ 10 %; 4.3.2.6 LBT/DAA with threshold −70 dBm/MHz + 10 log(100 mW/P). Clauses 4.3.2.4–4.3.2.6 do not apply **below 10 dBm EIRP**. Adaptive FHSS (4.3.1.4.3.2) needs ≥ 15 hop frequencies and ≤ 400 ms per frequency per 6 s. Audio routes give no more power at 2.4 GHz: IR2030/26/5 (wireless audio, EN 300 422) and 1/22 (non-specific) both allow 10 mW EIRP. The only higher-power body-worn audio band, IR2030/35/3 (1785–1805 MHz, 50 mW), is not a band the nRF5340 can transmit in. Our numbers (`audit.py` Audit 3): 212 µs per copy, so duty cycle 42.4 % per pack (73.6 % with TD). MU at 20 dBm is 42.4 % (TD off) or 73.6 % (TD on); MU reaches 10 % at 13.7 / 11.3 dBm. The longest silence in a frame is 436 / 124 µs, against the 3.5 ms Tx-gap rule. The worst 1 MHz of BLE 2M GFSK holds 76 % of its power, so PSD alone caps any non-FHSS mode at 11.2 dBm.
- **Decision:** UK is the default region (`host/channel_plan.yaml` `region: UK`). The pack is **non-adaptive non-FHSS with a declared max of 9 dBm EIRP**: +7 dBm conducted plus the 2 dBi whip, with 1 dB of margin under 10 dBm. US (FCC 15.247, 20 dBm EIRP) stays as a region setting on the same hardware, and firmware clamps power by region. **Keep the nRF21540 FEM:** +7 dBm conducted needs it, since the nRF5340 alone tops out at +3 dBm, so the FEM buys 4 dB. Run it at low gain (~38 mA at +10 dBm vs 110 mA at +20). BOM delta $0. Dropping to the BT40E (u.FL, no FEM, $9.55) would save $3.92/pack but cost those 4 dB of R1 margin. **Receiver LNA:** the region does not regulate it. Behind the rack LNA (RK-2) the module's nRF21540 LNA improves system NF by only 0.7 dB (8.6 vs 9.3 dB), so #32 may fit BT40E on the 8 rx modules, saving $31/rack at Fanstel prices. **Channel plan (#76):** carriers are ≥ 3 MHz apart. Seven gap carriers (2423 2426 2448 2451 2473 2476 2479) go one each to packs 2–8. Pack 1 (2414/2456) has no gap carrier, and every other carrier sits ≥ 5 MHz from it. Whichever pack stands next to an antenna, every other pack keeps a carrier ≥ 5 MHz away. The validator (`host/channel_plan.py`) and `tests/test_host.py` enforce this. The same plan serves UK Wi-Fi 1/6/11. No static plan has gap carriers under 1/5/9/13. Under 1/6/11 plus 13, packs 6–8 lose theirs.
- **Evidence (bsim, `tests/bsim/scenarios/run.py` from PR #82 (`issue-27-scenarios`, 2026-10-31), CI lengths: `run.py --only bc --plan host/channel_plan.yaml --eirp {9,14,17,20} [--wifi 1,5,9,13 | 1,6,11,13]`; Wi-Fi 40 % set at 20 dBm, 2 m; near pack 1):** At **US 20 dBm**, scenario (c) passes every check: no cross-pack loss with pack 1 near, and SYS-3 for all 8 packs with TD on (TD-length packets 0.000–0.021 % loss; TD off v1 packets 0.19–0.60 %, failing 0.1 %). This closes #76 for the US region. At **UK 9 dBm** with 1/6/11, carriers 11–12 MHz from a Wi-Fi centre lose 33–47 % of copies, and packs 2–6 fail SYS-3 (TD on 8.7–24.8 %). Packs 7/8 (2476/2479, ≥ 14 MHz away) pass with 0.000 %. The same carriers lose 42–55 % at 14 dBm and 33–38 % at 17 dBm, and recover only at ~20 dBm. With 1/5/9/13, or with 1/6/11 plus 13, every pack fails except the near pack (TD on 8.8–40 %). The analytic C/I with the 802.11 mask agrees: +5 dB vs the 10 dB needed (`audit.py`). Pack 1 passes (c) only because it is the near pack. At 10 m both its copies are in-band, as in (b) `inband`: at 40 % busy, TD on gives 4.7 % loss (v1 length) and 8.8 % (TD length), in either region. **So R1 + R9 already fail for one pack in the US.** Seven gap carriers at 3 MHz cannot serve eight packs.

- **Consequences:** No in-scope UK option meets R1 under the ADR-0003 geometry (Wi-Fi at 2 m from the receive antenna, pack at 10 m). That is a **stop-and-ask, reasons 4 and 5**. Nearest achievable numbers: in bsim, 11 MHz gap carriers need ~11 dB more isolation than UK power allows. That means Wi-Fi ≥ ~7 m from the receive antenna (or behind a directional antenna with ≥ 11 dB front-to-back), or the pack within ~3 m. Otherwise only packs whose gap carrier is ≥ 14 MHz from a Wi-Fi centre (2 of 8) meet SYS-3. The venue's own Wi-Fi must stay on 1/6/11 and off 13. Phase 5 measures real phones' leakage at 11–17 MHz offset, since the bsim WLAN spectrum is −12…−15 dB at 11 MHz where the 802.11 mask allows −20. Default while waiting: develop and gate at both 9 and 20 dBm.
