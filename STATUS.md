Phase: 2 of 6 (firmware in the simulator)
Done since last update: The two rack antennas now go in the wings (your rule): one per side, 4 m up on a lighting boom, aimed across the stage at the far back corner. All 8 packs still pass in simulation with the house full, including actors waiting in both wings (docs/human/antenna-install.md). The 8 receivers switch to a simpler, cheaper radio module (about $58 less per rack). Clock sync between the body pack and the rack. All five simulated tests pass with the new UK radio plan. UK radio rules are worked out and built in. The delay from microphone to output is fixed at 2.3 ms. Both circuit-board schematics are done. A UK parts list is done. Fixed a rare glitch in the pack (#87): when another part of the pack's software woke up at the wrong moment, one thousandth of a second of sound was never sent. The pack now sends real microphone sound through the real compression step, timed by the audio itself instead of a software alarm clock. In the simulator, under heavy extra load, it sends every sound frame in order and never sends one twice (before: 1 in 140 frames went out twice). Every change now runs this test (ADR-0020). Time diversity (resending each sound a moment later) is now a real per-pack setting (#91). A pack is switched with `td on` over its USB cable, and the receiver works it out by itself. In the simulator, a pack with no clear channel under 40 % audience Wi-Fi drops from 21.9 % lost sound to 8.9 %. At 10 % Wi-Fi it drops from 1.6 % to 0.09 %. With it on, sound arrives 4.58 thousandths of a second after the mic (limit 5). It is still off by default (2.65). The pack now sends each sound as soon as it is ready (#98): it used to wait 0.4 ms for the radio. Measured in the simulator from the moment the sound is complete, mic to output is 2.67 ms (2.28 ms with the low-delay filter on the receiver board, target 3); with time diversity on, 4.60 ms (limit 5). On the real board the pack's audio clock runs very slightly fast against its radio clock. Without a fix, about every 45 minutes one thousandth of a second of sound would be lost. A small correction loop now keeps the two in step (#97, ADR-0022). In the simulator, with the clock set 0.37 or even 40 millionths fast or slow, it lost no sound in an hour. The real audio hardware path is first tried on the bench (Phase 5). The rack receiver boards now use the cheaper BT40E radio module with no built-in amplifier (#112, ADR-0021): about £24 less per rack, and it cannot be overloaded by a pack standing next to a wing antenna.
