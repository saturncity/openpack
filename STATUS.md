Phase: 4 of 6 (hardware design, all checked on the computer before anything is bought). About 92% done.
Done since last update: The firmware for our own two boards (pack and receiver) is merged (PR #119). Pin assignments are generated from the circuit netlists and checked automatically, with no conflicts. Real audio hardware drivers now run on both boards. The pack switches itself off after 5 seconds below 3.3 V. The start-up loader no longer uses an insecure test random-number generator; boards now use ECDSA-P256 signing (PR #121). The signing key is still the public development key, which is open risk (5) in the design log. A design log now records every design difficulty and choice (docs/design-log.md, PR #123): 115 entries, and an "Open risks for audit" table of 16 items. Every design change must update it. The wing-antenna plan now meets the dropout goal in simulation. The receiver now uses the simpler BT40E radio module. The pack board is fully laid out (17.5 hour battery, $33.34 each). The delay from microphone to output is now 2.3 ms. The pack's clock drift is corrected. The receiver board is laid out (48 × 90 mm) and passes the board-maker's rules; its parts list has a real stock number for every part except the radio module. The rack board is laid out (412 × 323 mm, four layers) and passes the board-maker's rules; it fits the 1U case with 3.4 mm under the lid, and its radio paths are drawn to the right impedance. Its antenna splitter loses more than first planned (13.7 dB; the limit is now 14 dB), but the receivers still have 8.2 dB of noise margin. The UART clock crystal is now a sturdier part (60 ohm, rated −40 to +85 °C).

Risks: (1) The dropout margin is thin (0.086 % against a 0.1 % worst-case limit), so the install rule must be followed: one antenna per wing, about 4 m up on a boom, aimed at the far upstage corner; no Wi-Fi in the wings or on stage; crew phones in airplane mode.
Risks: (2) The 2482 MHz carrier of pack 1 has not been simulated; it passes by equivalence, pending a measured occupied-bandwidth check.
Risks: (3) Bench calibration happens after the boards are designed, so a board re-spin is possible.
Risks: (4) Two pack amplifier settings (the antenna-select level and the amplifier mode pin) are unverified and can only be confirmed on the bench.
Risks: (5) Module certification paperwork is incomplete: the BT40NE whip is not on the FCC grant, and no UK or EU declaration has been published for the modules.
Risks: (6) The side rails of the rack case may touch the edge of the rack board; a test fit of the bare board settles it, and the edge can be notched if needed.
