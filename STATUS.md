Phase: 2 of 6 (firmware in the simulator)
Done since last update: Fixed a rare glitch in the pack (#87): when another part of the pack's software woke up at the wrong moment, one thousandth of a second of sound never got sent. The pack now sends real microphone sound through the real compression step, timed by the audio itself instead of a software alarm clock. In the simulator, under heavy extra load, it sends every sound frame, in order, and none twice (before: 1 in 140 sent twice). This is tested on every change (ADR-0020).
Next: build time diversity as a per-pack setting (#91); finish the remaining simulator tests (#27).
Waiting on human: (1) when Phase 5 starts, buy 2 antennas and their cables, £340.56 ex VAT from ITM Components UK; the list and links are in issue #92. (2) Install KiCad (the circuit-board program): type `sudo dnf install kicad`. Not urgent, needed before board layout.
Risks: The real antenna rejects sound from behind less well than the study assumed (about 9 instead of 15 "decibels", a measure of loudness). So the rule is stricter: Wi-Fi gear must stay at least 4.5 m from the antennas (not 3 m), and the antennas must be at least 6 m up. If a venue can't do that, switch the nearest packs to time diversity. New: the sound waits about 0.4 thousandths of a second longer inside the pack than our budget counts, so the true delay is about 3.1 instead of 2.65 (limit 5); a fix to send it sooner is planned (#98). On the real board the audio clock and radio clock differ very slightly; a small correction loop is planned for the bench phase (#97).
